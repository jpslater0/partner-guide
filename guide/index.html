What it is

Four things happen in one room.

A channel is the artist's permanent home. Fans join it once and stay. It holds every show, every host and every drop in one place. Open a live channel

Fans host the shows. They play the music, they run the chat, they keep the room warm between releases. The artist does not have to be there for the room to be full. See a fan-hosted room

When the artist picks up the mic, they talk over the music to the people already in the room. No production, no set build. A phone is enough. See a channel that has had the artist on mic

A shop sits inside the room while the music plays. Fans buy without leaving the conversation. See a channel with a shop

Why it works

Every play is a real stream.

Fans connect their own paid streaming account. When the room plays a song, it plays on each person's own account. One room, thousands of individual streams, all of them counted where they matter.

That is why a listening party here moves numbers instead of just moving attention.

Proof

35,058 people in one room.

One listening party, one night in August. 171,040 streams played from 13,796 separate accounts.

51,138 chat messages across the show. At the peak, 2,547 of them landed inside a single minute.

A J-pop group, 24 August 2026. Event listeners counted as signed in users plus guests.

Proof

The artist typed instead of talking.

A smaller room the same night, 156 people. The artist never picked up the mic. They sat in the chat and sent 79 messages themselves.

The room played 3,810 streams and sent 1,860 messages back. Turning up is the whole thing. It does not have to be produced.

A J-pop group, 24 August 2026.

Proof

The room fills before you arrive.

An anniversary week for a K-pop group. Around 7,000 fans in the station, with no member of the group on the mic at any point. Another group's room ran alongside it with around 6,000.

Fans host. That is the default, not the fallback. When an artist does walk in, they are walking into something already running.
